---

A **part** of your app is a server process or a scheduled job that must keep running: your API, a worker, the outbox relay, a nightly import. Each part sends a small sign of life, a **heartbeat**, every 5 minutes.

Heartbeats never count toward your monthly events, and they keep arriving when your limit is reached.

## Where you see them

Turn on the [developer view](/using/developer-view), open **Settings** in the side bar of your app, then the tab **Developer** and the row **Heartbeats and reachability**: "Is Acme alive? Two separate checks: your app reports in, and everydeck calls it from outside." It needs a role that may see code: Owner, Admin, Agency or Viewer.

**Heartbeats from your app** shows:

- how many instances report and how often, like "One instance reports every 5 min · last one 2 min ago",
- one word for the state: **OK**, **Late** or **Silent**,
- under **Parts of your app, last 12 hours**, one row per part: a server instance that sends heartbeats, or a job from your app map with its name, the last sign, and one mark per hour for the last 12 hours. An hour stays empty if the part sent nothing in it.

If your app sent no heartbeat yet, it says "No heartbeat yet. Your app sends one as soon as its server starts with everydeck."

The overview shows only the sum in its footer: how many programs are alive, how many schedules are on time, and the last sign. See [Overview](/using/overview#footer).

### Late and silent

| State | When |
|---|---|
| **Late** | Later than one period plus 15 seconds. |
| **Silent** | After two periods plus 30 seconds. With 5 minutes, that is 10 minutes and 30 seconds. |

After a restart or a deploy, the old instances are replaced. They do not count as an outage and are not listed.

### A heartbeat is no proof of the whole app

The same page keeps two statements apart:

- **Missing heartbeats** are a monitoring gap, not an outage.
- A **confirmed outage** needs a failed [reachability check](/using/reachability-checks): "Only two failed calls in a row count as an outage."

A heartbeat from your backend does not prove a working database behind it.

## When a part goes silent

The gap shows on the page right away. A case or an email only follows if it lasts 5 more minutes after everydeck noticed it. A gap that ends by itself before that never reports.

- **One part goes silent** while the rest still sends: you get one email and a banner. It ends when the part sends again.
- **The whole app goes silent:** a case opens, "Your app stopped sending signals". In production the email comes after 15 minutes without a heartbeat. The case closes by itself once your app sends again for 5 minutes without a break: "Your app sends signals again."
- **A scheduled job misses two periods:** a case opens. It closes when the job reports again. This runs in production only. See [Schedules](/using/schedules).

## How scheduled jobs report

Your coding agent adds the heartbeat to each job it finds. You never add a key, a secret or a variable for a schedule. There are three ways.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Jobs in your code">
    The job sends its heartbeat at the end of each run that worked:

    ```ts
    everydeck.heartbeat(3600, { job: "nightly_import" });
    ```

    The same holds for a schedule outside your app that only calls a route of your own server, for example a GitHub Actions workflow or a cron at your host that sends a request to a cron URL. The heartbeat goes into that route. Nothing is added to the schedule itself.

    See the [JavaScript SDK](/developers/sdk-javascript#heartbeats) or the [Python SDK](/developers/sdk-python#heartbeats-of-jobs).
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Schedules in SQL (pg_cron)">
    The command runs inside everydeck's outbox, so the heartbeat is written in the same transaction:

    ```sql
    select cron.schedule('nightly_import', '0 3 * * *',
      $$ select everydeck_outbox.run_job('nightly_import', $job$ call import_listings() $job$) $$);
    ```

    A failed command rolls back its writes and reports `error`. No key is in the SQL. The outbox relay delivers the heartbeat.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Workflows that run the job themselves">
    A GitHub Actions workflow that runs the job's code itself ends with a check-in step. See [below](#workflows-that-run-the-job-themselves).
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

A heartbeat can carry a result: `ok`, `error`, or `in_progress` at the start. On the [Setup page](/using/setup-page), a job is **Working** only after a run that worked, and **Last run failed** after a failed one.

If a job really cannot send a heartbeat, Setup says "everydeck can’t watch this job" instead of waiting for data.

### Workflows that run the job themselves

GitHub signs who runs the workflow, so no secret, no variable and no key is needed. Your agent gives the job the permission `id-token: write` and adds a last step:

```yaml
- name: everydeck check-in
  if: always()
  continue-on-error: true
  env:
    STATUS: ${{ job.status == 'success' && 'ok' || 'error' }}
  run: |
    TOKEN=$(curl -fsS -H "Authorization: bearer $ACTIONS_ID_TOKEN_REQUEST_TOKEN" "$ACTIONS_ID_TOKEN_REQUEST_URL&audience=<everydeck address>" | jq -r .value)
    curl -fsS --max-time 10 -X POST "<everydeck address>/v1/jobs/nightly_import/check-in?status=$STATUS&project=<project id>" -H "authorization: Bearer $TOKEN"
```

Your agent writes in the everydeck address and the id of your app. Neither is secret. A failed check-in never fails your workflow. The check-in accepts only a job from your approved app map, and only from the repository of your app.

## When you remove everydeck

Every schedule gets its own command back before everydeck removes the outbox. If that is not safely possible, everydeck removes nothing and names the schedule. See [Removing everydeck](/reference/remove-everydeck).

## What there is not

- No history of parts longer than 12 hours. Schedules keep 30 days on [Schedules](/using/schedules).
- No parts without an SDK heartbeat.
- A failed job run opens no case of its own. Only a silent job does.
