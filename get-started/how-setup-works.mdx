title: "How setup works"
description: "What happens during the everydeck setup: what your agent does, what you see on the approval page, what everydeck adds to your Supabase project and what it never reads."
---

The setup has three parts, shown at the top of the setup page as **Start**, **Your OK** and **Live**. Your coding agent reads your app. You approve. Then your agent adds everydeck to your code, and everydeck waits until your app runs it.

## What your agent does

After you paste the sentence, your agent works through everydeck's steps one by one. everydeck tells it each step. You can watch on the setup page. While your agent reads, the page says **Claude Code is reading Acme** (with the name of your tool) and lists:

- **Started**, with the time
- **Reading the code and the database structure**: no rows and no keys, only names of tables, columns and places in the code. The page counts what was found so far: programs, tables, ways through the app and scheduled jobs.
- **Your OK**: one page with everything everydeck asks for.
- **Claude Code builds it in and opens a pull request**

Your agent already writes the measuring points into its own copy of the code while it reads. Nothing goes to your repository before your OK.

If the page knows how long setups take, it says about how many minutes. That number comes only from setups that really finished, never from a guess. You can close the page; the setup keeps going without you. The page checks for news every 15 seconds. If your agent has not checked in for 5 minutes, the page says when it last did. That is normal: agents often read for a while without checking in.

The card of your app on **Your apps** shows the same progress, for example **Reading your app** and **Step 2 of 4**.

The sentence you paste is:

```text
Set up everydeck for this app, including the read-only connection that lets everydeck read the data I approve, restart the local servers of this app when needed, and open a pull request, or commit locally if the repository has no remote.
```

This sentence is your consent to three things: the read-only connection to your data, restarting your app's own local servers, and the pull request. Your agent pushes only its own branch. It never merges or deploys.

<Note>
  Lovable saves code by itself and has no local servers, so it gets a shorter sentence. See [Lovable](/connect/lovable).
</Note>

### The app map

Your agent writes down the facts of your app in a file called the **app map**: tables, pages, sign-in, payments, background jobs, AI calls, and the flows people go through. Every fact has its place in your code. everydeck checks the app map before it shows it to you.

If your agent left something out, everydeck gives it back to the agent first, the most important parts first (money, scheduled jobs, email and push, sign-in, ways through the app). It sends at most 15 open parts at once.

### Rules your agent follows

- It never signs up, signs in or creates an account in your app. Not live, not in development, not with a test address.
- It never writes test records into your database. It proves the setup with test events that are marked as a probe run and count nowhere.
- It never reads files with secrets, like `.env` files. It checks variables by their name only.
- It never takes a key from your computer to everydeck. everydeck creates every key itself and shows it once. Your agent writes it into your app's local env file, which git ignores.
- It never operates your browser or the everydeck dashboard. Approving is your click.

Flows behind the sign-in wait for **the first real sign-in**. everydeck confirms them by itself when real people use them.

## The approval page

When your agent has read your app, the setup page says **Acme is ready for your OK**. This is everything everydeck asks for, on one page:

- **What everydeck reads**: each kind of data, for example **Users** and listings, with its fields. Read only. You can change this later under **Settings › Data and privacy**. A switch **Text people write** decides whether everydeck shows free text on the page of each record. Phone numbers, IP addresses and e-mail addresses stay in your app.
- **People**: if some people in your app have no name, you can turn on **Show the e-mail when someone has no name**. Without it they show as "Someone". everydeck then reads only the e-mail column of the users, nothing else.
- **Messages**: if your app keeps messages, **Count messages and show failed ones**. everydeck sees that a message went out, to which person (never the address) and whether it arrived.
- **Money**: if your app bills with a payment provider, choose **Connect it** or **Skip**. See [Payments in the setup](#payments-in-the-setup).
- **Code**: GitHub, read only: pull requests, checks, deploys. If GitHub is not connected, the page says **Still open: GitHub is not connected.** You can still approve. Without GitHub, everydeck sees the new version of your app only when it first reports.
- **What Claude Code changes in Acme**: the server, the web app, scheduled jobs and, with Supabase, a read-only function and its schedule that everydeck adds itself.
- **What everydeck can change**: what your team may change, if your app is on Supabase. For example edit and archive listings, or send a sign-in link. See [Changing data](/using/changing-data).

If your agent and everydeck see something differently, the page asks you first. **Approve** works once you have answered.

Click **Approve**. Approving changes nothing in your app yet. Your agent opens a pull request, and you merge it. If you approved changes from everydeck and you set up a second factor, everydeck asks for its code first.

<Info>
  E-mail addresses and phone numbers that everydeck finds inside anything else it reads are removed before it stores or shows them. What your app itself sends to everydeck, for example a person's e-mail when someone signs in or the text of an error, everydeck keeps as your app sends it.
</Info>

## What everydeck adds to your Supabase project

If you connected Supabase, everydeck adds its own parts to your project after you approve: database changes that only add, functions and their settings. Nothing of your app is changed or removed.

Depending on your app, these parts are:

| Part | What it is for |
|---|---|
| Function `everydeck-read-bridge` | Reads the data you approved, read only. |
| Function `everydeck-outbox-relay` | Only if your browser writes straight into the database. Sends confirmed results to everydeck. |
| Function `everydeck-write-door` | Only if you approved changes. Makes the changes you switched on, and nothing else. |
| Schemas `everydeck_outbox`, `everydeck_door`, `everydeck_read` | Hold everydeck's own tables and views. |
| Role `everydeck_reader` | A database role that can only read. |
| Function `public.everydeck_server_config()` | Lets your own server read its everydeck settings. Only `service_role` may run it. |
| Schedules, Vault entries and function settings | everydeck creates the values itself. They never go to your agent or into the chat. |

The setup page shows **everydeck is adding its parts to** your project, then **everydeck added its parts to** your project. If it stops, click **Try again**.

You can remove all of this later. See [Removing everydeck from your app](/reference/remove-everydeck).

<Warning>
  If you deploy everydeck's functions again yourself with the Supabase CLI, Supabase turns its JWT check back on for them, and everydeck can no longer reach them.
</Warning>

## What everydeck never reads

- **No rows through the Supabase connection.** everydeck reads only the structure: names of tables and columns.
- **No contact columns.** Phone and IP address columns are never in the read scope. E-mail columns are not either, unless you turn on **Show the e-mail when someone has no name**. Then everydeck reads only the e-mail column of the users.
- **No message texts.** everydeck reads the text of a message only when someone on your team opens it with a reason, and it keeps nothing. See [Moderation](/using/moderation).
- **No `.env` values, passwords or card numbers.** everydeck never stores them.
- **No secret keys of Supabase.** everydeck needs no public Supabase key either.

## Two environments

Every app has two environments: **development** and **production**. If your app is on a connected Supabase project, the setup writes to production. Otherwise it writes to development, and going live is your step later. See [Apps on GitHub and a host](/connect/github-and-hosting).

## Payments in the setup

If your app bills with a payment provider and you choose **Connect it** on the approval page, your agent adds payment events where your app gets the confirmation of a payment. everydeck checks them with a test event before the setup ends. No real payment happens. Payments count as connected once everydeck receives the first real one. Until then, Revenue stays "not connected". If you choose **Skip**, everydeck shows plans and trials, and Revenue stays "not connected".

## When the setup ends

The setup ends without follow-up work for you. With a pull request, the setup page stays at **Almost live**:

- **Merge pull request #12**: click **Open on GitHub** and merge it. Notes from your agent stand above it: read them before you merge.
- **Deploy**: your host starts by itself after the merge. If a part has no new version 10 minutes after the merge, the page says how to deploy it again at that host.
- **Every part reports in**: the server, the web app and scheduled jobs. A job that has not run yet does not hold this up.

Tools that commit to the default branch themselves, like Lovable, get the same page with that commit instead of the pull request. See [Lovable](/connect/lovable).

When every part of your app runs the new version, your app is live. everydeck emails you once that Acme is live. Like other setup emails, it comes in the daily summary unless you chose **Right away**. See [Emails and notifications](/using/emails-and-notifications).

If everydeck finds a part after the merge that it had not seen before, the page asks you to decide whether your app has it. This does not hold up going live.

Without a pull request the setup ends in one of three ways:

- your agent reports that it is done,
- your app sent real data after your approval,
- you click **End this setup** on the setup page. See [Setup page](/using/setup-page#end-this-setup).

The setup page then shows **Acme is on deck** and **Open Acme**. If something is left for you, it stands in one box, **Your turn**, for example **What everydeck found** with the open parts. Under **More about the setup** the page lists **Notes from the setup** and **First real use**.

## Updates later

When everydeck has a newer version of its parts for your app, or a part of your app does not report, the [Setup page](/using/setup-page) shows **Update available**. Click **Update** and paste the sentence into your coding agent. Your agent builds the update as a pull request, the same way as the setup. Nothing changes in your app until you merge it. Then everydeck checks every part again. A fix for a security gap shows as **Security update available**.

If your agent later refines what it found, everydeck takes it over without asking you again. Anything that would let everydeck see or do more, for example an update that reads more data, comes back to you for a new approval.
