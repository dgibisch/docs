title: "How setup works"
description: "What happens during the everydeck setup: what your agent does, what you see on the approval page, what everydeck adds to your Supabase project and what it never reads."
---

The setup has three parts. Your coding agent reads your app. You approve. Then your agent adds everydeck to your code.

## What your agent does

After you paste the sentence, your agent works through everydeck's steps one by one. everydeck tells it each step. You can watch on the setup page:

- **Connected to** your tool
- **Reading your app**
- **You approved**, with the time
- **Adding everydeck to your code**
- **Testing everything**

The page shows which step your agent is on and how many minutes are left. The minutes come only from setups that really finished, never from a guess. The page checks for news every 15 seconds.

The sentence you paste is:

```text
Set up everydeck for this app, including the read-only connection that lets everydeck read the data I approve, restart the local servers of this app when needed, and open a pull request, or commit locally if the repository has no remote.
```

This sentence is your consent to three things: the read-only connection to your data, restarting your app's own local servers, and the pull request. Your agent pushes only its own branch. It never merges or deploys.

<Note>
  Lovable saves code by itself and has no local servers, so it gets a shorter sentence. See [Lovable](/connect/lovable).
</Note>

### The app map

Your agent writes down the facts of your app in a file called the **app map**: tables, pages, sign-in, payments, background jobs, AI calls, and the flows people go through. Every fact has its place in your code. everydeck checks the app map before it shows it to you.

If your agent left something out, everydeck gives it back to the agent first, the most important parts first (money, scheduled jobs, email and push, sign-in, ways through the app). It sends at most 15 open parts at once.

### Rules your agent follows

- It never signs up, signs in or creates an account in your app. Not live, not in development, not with a test address.
- It never writes test records into your database. It proves the setup with test events that are marked as a probe run and count nowhere.
- It never reads files with secrets, like `.env` files. It checks variables by their name only.
- It never takes a key from your computer to everydeck. everydeck creates every key itself and shows it once. Your agent writes it into your app's local env file, which git ignores.
- It never operates your browser or the everydeck dashboard. Approving is your click.

Flows behind the sign-in wait for **the first real sign-in**. everydeck confirms them by itself when real people use them.

## The approval page

When your agent has read your app, the setup page says **Your turn: one click**. The box shows:

- **everydeck sees**: the kinds of data everydeck will show, for example people, orders and listings, plus errors and how the app runs.
- **Not read by everydeck**: e-mail addresses, phone numbers, WhatsApp numbers, IP addresses and messages stay in your app. everydeck does not read these columns.
- **You can change from everydeck**: what your team may change, if your app is on Supabase. For example edit and archive listings, or send a sign-in link. See [Changing data](/using/changing-data).
- **everydeck adds to** your Supabase project: if Supabase is connected, the exact parts everydeck adds after you approve.

Click **Approve**. You do not need a second factor for this click.

<Info>
  E-mail addresses and phone numbers that everydeck finds inside anything else it reads are removed before it stores or shows them. What your app itself sends to everydeck, for example a person's e-mail when someone signs in or the text of an error, everydeck keeps as your app sends it.
</Info>

## What everydeck adds to your Supabase project

If you connected Supabase, everydeck adds its own parts to your project after you approve: database changes that only add, functions and their settings. The approval box counts them and lets you open each file. Nothing of your app is changed or removed.

Depending on your app, these parts are:

| Part | What it is for |
|---|---|
| Function `everydeck-read-bridge` | Reads the data you approved, read only. |
| Function `everydeck-outbox-relay` | Only if your browser writes straight into the database. Sends confirmed results to everydeck. |
| Function `everydeck-write-door` | Only if you approved changes. Makes the changes you switched on, and nothing else. |
| Schemas `everydeck_outbox`, `everydeck_door`, `everydeck_read` | Hold everydeck's own tables and views. |
| Role `everydeck_reader` | A database role that can only read. |
| Function `public.everydeck_server_config()` | Lets your own server read its everydeck settings. Only `service_role` may run it. |
| Schedules, Vault entries and function settings | everydeck creates the values itself. They never go to your agent or into the chat. |

The setup page shows **everydeck is adding its parts to** your project, then **everydeck added its parts to** your project. If it stops, click **Try again**.

You can remove all of this later. See [Removing everydeck from your app](/reference/remove-everydeck).

<Warning>
  If you deploy everydeck's functions again yourself with the Supabase CLI, Supabase turns its JWT check back on for them, and everydeck can no longer reach them.
</Warning>

## What everydeck never reads

- **No rows through the Supabase connection.** everydeck reads only the structure: names of tables and columns.
- **No contact columns.** E-mail, phone, messenger and IP address columns are never in the read scope.
- **No message texts.** A chat is read live only when someone on your team opens it for a reason, and it is not stored. See [Moderation](/using/moderation).
- **No `.env` values, passwords or card numbers.** everydeck never stores them.
- **No secret keys of Supabase.** everydeck needs no public Supabase key either.

## Two environments

Every app has two environments: **development** and **production**. If your app is on a connected Supabase project, the setup writes to production. Otherwise it writes to development, and going live is your step later. See [Apps on GitHub and a host](/connect/github-and-hosting).

## When the setup ends

The setup ends in one of three ways:

- your agent reports that it is done,
- your app sent real data after your approval,
- you click **End this setup** on the setup page. See [Setup page](/using/setup-page#end-this-setup).

The setup page then shows **Done** and **Open Acme**, even if your app is not merged yet.

The end page lists **Notes from the setup**, **First real use** and, if something is missing, **What everydeck found** with the open parts. Read the notes before you merge.
